What this means for you: your first six weeks

For most patients, recovery looks something like this.

  1. Surgery dayYou’re awake under local anesthesia, then walk out in a soft cast and sandal, without crutches.
  2. Days 2 to 3Most people can drive once they feel steady in a shoe Dr. Tagge approves.
  3. Weeks 1 to 3You see Dr. Tagge once a week, and he adjusts the plan as you go.
  4. Weeks 2 to 4You move from the sandal into a supportive athletic shoe.
  5. Weeks 4 to 6You’re back in your own shoes.

Timelines vary from person to person. Walking isn’t the same as being fully healed.

But walking from day one does mean you’re not stuck on the couch asking for rides for weeks.

Questions people ask before they decide

Why haven’t I heard of this before?

Not every surgeon offers minimally invasive bunion surgery, and the term can mean different things from one surgeon to the next.

Dr. Tagge’s surgical practice is focused only on minimally invasive forefoot surgery, and PRISM is his own system.

Is minimally invasive surgery as thorough as open surgery?

Yes, when it’s done well. The openings are small, but Dr. Tagge makes the full correction your foot needs.

He also chooses the least disruptive correction that does the job well. More surgery isn’t automatically better surgery.

Will it hurt?

Expect some pain and swelling. Dr. Tagge plans how your pain will be managed as part of your recovery.

Can my bunion come back?

It can, after any bunion surgery. Lowering the chance is part of your plan, and Dr. Tagge will go over your own risk at the consultation.

Is my bunion too severe, or am I too old?

Not necessarily. Even significant bunions can often be corrected with minimally invasive surgery, and age alone doesn’t rule you out.

Dr. Tagge will tell you what’s possible for your foot.

Can my hammertoes be fixed at the same time?

Often, yes, when it makes sense for your foot. Dr. Tagge will tell you at the consultation.

Can both feet be done at once?

Rarely. Dr. Tagge prefers you always have one stable foot, so the second is usually done 2 to 3 weeks later.

Do you take insurance?

No, the practice doesn’t bill insurance.

Insurance decides what it will pay for. Dr. Tagge decided what your care should include, then built his practice to offer it.

Some patients file for out-of-network benefits themselves, and the practice can provide the paperwork. Coverage isn’t guaranteed.

What does it cost?

The cost depends on the plan Dr. Tagge recommends for your foot.

If surgery is right for you, you get one all-inclusive quote before you decide. The quote covers the surgery, the surgical center and your follow-up care, with no separate bills.

The consultation fee is shown when you pick a time.
