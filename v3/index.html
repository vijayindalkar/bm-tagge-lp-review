HOW DR. TAGGE APPROACHES SURGERY

Plan the Recovery Before the First Incision

Dr. Tagge starts with what your foot is keeping you from doing. From there, he uses PRISM™ to map out surgery and healing as one plan, in three stages:

  1. 01ROOT

    Understand the whole problem

    Look past the visible bump to the whole forefoot: how it moves, your overall health, and the activities you want back.

  2. 02CORRECT

    Keep the surgery as small as it can be

    Dr. Tagge realigns the joint through tiny incisions. A hammertoe or bunionette (a smaller bunion near the little toe) can be included in the same recovery.

  3. 03RECOVER

    Prepare for healing, then guide it

    Recovery planning starts before surgery day. Afterward, Dr. Tagge plans protection and follow-up visits as your activity builds.

Surgery still carries risks. Results and recovery vary, and some patients need temporary walking aids or a different approach.

Go deeper: the five parts of PRISM™

Inside those three stages, PRISM™ works through five parts.

  1. P

    Precision minimally invasive correction

    The structure is corrected through tiny incisions that limit damage to nearby tissue, and the correction still has to be accurate.

  2. R

    Regenerative biologic support

    Biologic support can be built into the recovery plan. The type and level depend on the patient.

  3. I

    Intrinsic healing optimization

    Health factors that affect healing are reviewed, and anything that can be improved is addressed before surgery.

  4. S

    Surgical correction stability

    The correction is protected while it heals, with a plan that balances stability and safe movement.

  5. M

    Mechanical optimization

    The plan looks at how your whole forefoot bears weight and moves, so it accounts for life after the bone has healed.

BEFORE YOU DECIDE

Answers to the Questions That Keep Surgery on Hold

Does Completing the Survey Mean I’m a Surgical Candidate?

It’s the first check. The survey shows whether the practice fits your needs. Dr. Tagge confirms whether you’re a surgical candidate at the consultation, after the exam, the imaging, and a review of your history.

What If I’ve Already Been Told I Need Surgery?

Second opinions are welcome. Bring what you were told and what worries you about it. Dr. Tagge will examine your foot and explain whether he agrees or would take a different approach.

Can a Bunion and Hammertoe Be Corrected Together?

When it’s appropriate, yes. Dr. Tagge looks at the whole forefoot, including any hammertoe or bunionette, and related problems can be corrected in one plan with one recovery.

Will I Be Off My Feet?

In most cases, patients walk out the same day in a protective surgical sandal. That’s protected walking, not a return to every activity. Some patients still need temporary walking aids, and Dr. Tagge will explain what applies to you.

How Soon Can I Return to Work, Exercise, or Normal Shoes?

It depends on the correction, the swelling, how you heal, and what each activity demands. Dr. Tagge won’t promise a date, but bring your work schedule and goals to the consultation and he’ll map out a realistic timeline.

What Should I Know About Price and Insurance?

Bunion Master is cash-pay, and surgery isn’t billed to insurance. Out-of-network benefits may apply. Regenerative components aren’t covered by insurance. The consultation fee is on the booking page. If surgery is recommended, you’ll get one all-inclusive price, with no separate facility or anesthesia bills.

What If I’ve Waited Too Long or My Bunion Is Severe?

Severity is only one part of the decision. Joint condition, bone quality, overall health, and foot mechanics matter too. The consultation is where Dr. Tagge can tell you what’s possible for your foot.
