A DIFFERENT WAY TO PLAN BUNION SURGERY

Plan the Recovery Before the First Incision

Dr. Tagge starts with what the foot is keeping you from doing and what would make treatment worthwhile. Then he considers the correction and the recovery together.

  1. 01ROOT

    Understand the whole problem

    Look beyond the visible bump to the whole forefoot, how it moves, your health, and the activities you want back.

  2. 02FIX

    Make the correction the foot needs

    Choose the least disruptive method that can reliably achieve the needed correction. If a hammertoe or bunionette is part of the problem, consider it in the same plan.

  3. 03HEAL

    Prepare for and guide recovery

    Address healing needs before surgery and plan protection, recovery support, and follow-up as activity increases.

Surgery still carries risks. Protected walking doesn’t mean unrestricted activity or a fully healed foot. Results and recovery vary, and some patients need a different approach or temporary walking aids.

Go deeper: the five parts of PRISM™

The smaller incision is one part of PRISM™. Dr. Tagge also plans for the bone to heal, the correction to stay protected, and the foot to work under the demands of everyday life.

  1. P

    Precision minimally invasive correction

    Correct the structure through tiny incisions while limiting damage to nearby tissue. The correction still needs to be accurate.

  2. R

    Regenerative biologic support

    Consider biologic support as part of the recovery plan when appropriate. The type and level of support depend on the patient.

  3. I

    Intrinsic healing optimization

    Review health factors that affect healing and address what can be improved before surgery. Preparation is part of treatment.

  4. S

    Surgical correction stability

    Protect the correction while it heals, using a plan that balances stability with safe movement.

  5. M

    Mechanical optimization

    Consider how the whole forefoot bears weight and moves, so the plan accounts for life after the bone has healed.

PUTTING IT OFF DOESN’T MEAN YOU’VE BEEN CARELESS

The Options You’ve Heard About Deserve a Closer Look

A friend’s difficult recovery, advice from years ago, or an earlier surgery recommendation can stay with you. Those concerns are worth discussing, but they don’t tell you exactly what your foot needs today.

01

Relief Doesn’t Always Change the Structure

Wider shoes, spacers, and inserts can help manage pressure and discomfort. They don’t realign the bone. If daily life is becoming harder despite those measures, it’s reasonable to revisit the options.

02

“Minimally Invasive” Isn’t One Standard Operation

Small incisions don’t tell the whole story. The correction, hardware, anesthesia, and recovery can differ between procedures. Ask about those details before you choose.

03

Someone Else’s Recovery Isn’t Your Surgical Plan

Your foot, health, and responsibilities all matter. An exam and a full discussion can show which parts of the recovery you’ve imagined actually apply to your case.

BEFORE YOU DECIDE

Answers to the Questions That Keep Surgery on Hold

Does Completing the Survey Mean I’m a Surgical Candidate?

No. The survey checks whether the practice fits your needs and plans. Dr. Tagge determines clinical candidacy during the consultation, after the exam, imaging, and history review.

What If I’ve Already Been Told I Need Surgery?

Second opinions are welcome. Tell Dr. Tagge what was recommended and what concerns you. He’ll assess your foot and explain whether he agrees or would consider another approach.

Can a Bunion and Hammertoe Be Addressed Together?

The consultation looks at the whole forefoot, including a hammertoe or bunionette when present. When appropriate, related problems can be corrected in one plan and one recovery.

Will I Be Off My Feet?

In most cases, patients walk the same day in a protective surgical sandal. That’s protected walking, not a return to every activity. Some patients still need temporary walking aids, and Dr. Tagge will explain what applies to your correction.

How Soon Can I Return to Work, Exercise, or Normal Shoes?

That depends on the correction, swelling, healing, and what the activity requires. Dr. Tagge doesn’t promise a specific recovery date. Bring your work schedule and activity goals to the consultation so he can explain a realistic plan.

What Should I Know About Price and Insurance?

The practice is cash-pay, and surgery isn’t billed to insurance. Out-of-network benefits may apply. Regenerative components aren’t covered by insurance.

If your answers fit the practice, you’ll see the paid consultation’s fee and refund terms on the booking page before booking. If surgery is recommended, the practice provides an all-inclusive price for the plan, with no separate facility or anesthesia bills.

What If I’ve Waited Too Long or My Bunion Is Severe?

Severity is one part of the decision. Joint condition, bone quality, health, and foot mechanics matter too. The consultation is where Dr. Tagge can determine what’s possible for your foot.
